\title{Zero-Shot Large Audio-Language Models versus Trained Captioners:\\
A Preregistered Evaluation on Clotho with Polyphony and Hallucination Analyses}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Zuraiz}

Supervisor: Prof.\ Dr.-Ing.\ Jakob Abe{\ss}er}}

\begin{abstract}
Large audio-language models (LALMs) promise open-vocabulary audio understanding
without task-specific training, but whether they match trained audio captioners
remains unclear. We evaluate three zero-shot LALMs (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, SALMONN-13B,
Audio Flamingo 3) against three traditional systems (an AST tagging floor, the
DCASE 2023 CNN14+BART baseline, EnCLAP-base) on the full Clotho~v2.1 evaluation
split under one harness with identical data, decoding provenance, and DCASE
metrics. The CNN14 baseline reproduces its published score within 0.002
SPIDEr-FL, validating the harness; the EnCLAP row runs $\approx$0.011 below
its published value, a gap we report and examine. Zero-shot performance is strongly
model-dependent: two LALMs trail both trained captioners, while Audio Flamingo~3
surpasses them on every metric under the same harness (SPIDEr-FL 0.297 vs.\ 0.280, also
above EnCLAP's published 0.291; bootstrap CI excludes the preregistered 0.261
threshold). A PANNs-based polyphony split shows
\emph{higher} caption-overlap scores on event-rich polyphonic clips for all six
systems, and a closed-vocabulary hallucination metric (CHAIR-audio) shows the
best captioner does not hallucinate less per caption than its LALM peer,
although per mention it is the most grounded system in the table. ``Zero-shot''
denotes the evaluation protocol (no fine-tuning, no in-context examples); the
audio-specialist LALMs' training corpora include Clotho development pairs, a
caveat the paper examines. All hypotheses were
preregistered; deviations are disclosed.
Code: \url{https://github.com/Zuraiz270/audio-to-text-captioning-using-LALMs}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Automated audio captioning (AAC) maps an audio recording to a natural-language
description of its sound events. Traditional AAC systems are trained end-to-end
on caption corpora such as Clotho~\cite{drossos2020clotho}; large audio-language
models (LALMs) instead couple a pretrained audio encoder to a large language
model and are used \emph{zero-shot}.

The task is hard for two reasons. Real recordings rarely contain one clean
source: everyday scenes stack engines, voices, weather, and animals on top of
each other, and a captioner must both detect this mixture and verbalise it.
Evaluation is just as delicate, because reference-overlap metrics reward
phrasing that resembles five particular human sentences, which is not the same
thing as being acoustically right. Reliable captioning would nevertheless be
useful well beyond benchmarks, for indexing large sound archives, for urban and
environmental monitoring, and for making audio material accessible in text
form. Whether zero-shot LALMs already match
purpose-trained captioners, and where they fail, is the question this
project answers empirically. We ask:
\textbf{RQ1}: Do zero-shot LALMs match or exceed traditional captioning
baselines on standard metrics (SPIDEr-FL, CIDEr)?
\textbf{RQ2}: How does performance differ between polyphonic (overlapping
events) and monophonic clips?
\textbf{RQ3}: What are the failure modes, in particular caption hallucination?

Our contributions are: (i) a reproducible six-system comparison on the full
Clotho-eval split, anchored by a CNN14 reproduction that validates the harness;
(ii) a preregistered, bootstrap-based hypothesis-testing protocol with all
deviations disclosed; (iii) an SED-derived polyphony split and a
closed-vocabulary adaptation of the CHAIR hallucination
metric~\cite{rohrbach2018chair} to audio.
The paper reviews related work in Sec.~\ref{sec:related}, describes the
harness, the polyphony split, the hallucination metric, and the statistics in
Sec.~\ref{sec:method}, reports results per research question in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, and closes with discussion, limitations, and
conclusions in Secs.~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Datasets, systems, and metrics.}
Clotho~v2.1~\cite{drossos2020clotho} is the standard AAC benchmark: 1{,}045
evaluation clips (15--30\,s, 44.1\,kHz) with five human captions each. The
DCASE challenge series established SPIDEr~\cite{liu2017spider} and its
fluency-penalised variant SPIDEr-FL~\cite{zhou2022fense} as headline metrics;
SPIDEr-FL's fluency detector also underlies FENSE, the caption metric used
in~\cite{harish2025dcase}. We compute all metrics with
\texttt{aac-metrics}~\cite{labbe_aacmetrics}.
Trained captioners include the DCASE 2023 baseline (CNN14+BART, encoder from
PANNs~\cite{kong2020panns})~\cite{dcase2023task6a} and
EnCLAP~\cite{kim2024enclap}, whose released checkpoints were later re-measured
by its authors~\cite{kim2024enclappp}. AST~\cite{gong2021ast} is a 527-class
AudioSet~\cite{gemmeke2017audioset} tagger we use as a tagging floor.

\textbf{LALMs.} Qwen2.5-Omni~\cite{xu2025qwenomni} is a general
text/vision/audio/speech model; SALMONN~\cite{tang2024salmonn} fuses Whisper
and BEATs encoders through a Q-Former into a 13B LLM; Audio Flamingo~3
(AF3)~\cite{goel2025af3} pairs a Whisper-style encoder with a Qwen2-7B decoder
and is the current open state of the art for audio understanding. Together
they cover a general omni-model, an earlier audio specialist, and a current
audio specialist from three different vendors.

\textbf{Polyphony and hallucination.} For polyphonic scene understanding,
Abe{\ss}er et al.~\cite{abesser2023usm} introduced the USM dataset, and Harish
and Abe{\ss}er~\cite{harish2025dcase} proposed a three-level protocol (tagging,
counting and loudness ranking, captioning) on which LALM performance on
\emph{event-level} tasks degrades with increasing polyphony, a finding our
\emph{caption-level} RQ2 complements. CHAIR~\cite{rohrbach2018chair} measures
object hallucination in image captioning against a closed list;
MACE~\cite{dixit2024mace} scores audio captions against the audio itself via
CLAP embeddings.

Our study differs from these efforts in three ways. It evaluates on real
Clotho recordings rather than synthetic mixtures, so polyphony comes from the
world and not from a mixing script. It anchors the LALMs against trained
captioners reproduced inside the same harness, which turns cross-paper number
comparisons into one controlled measurement. And it commits to hypotheses,
thresholds, and fallback rules in advance and reports every deviation, which
is still uncommon in audio captioning evaluations.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Evaluation Harness}
Every system implements one contract, \texttt{caption(waveform, sr)
$\rightarrow$ str}, behind a registry, so a single inference loop and a
single scorer serve all rows. Each run writes a predictions JSON plus a
manifest recording checkpoint SHA-256s, library versions, decode parameters,
and the seed (42). Scoring uses the \texttt{dcase2023} preset of
\texttt{aac-metrics}~0.5.5 (SPIDEr-FL, CIDEr-D, SPICE, METEOR, fluency error)
on identical clip sets; a \texttt{--subset} mechanism restricts scoring to
fixed clip lists for same-set comparisons. Baselines ran on CPU; LALMs ran on
one NVIDIA A100-40GB each (offline, pre-cached checkpoints).

Runs are engineered for auditability. The inference loop flushes predictions
atomically every ten clips and isolates per-clip failures, so a crash loses at
most nine captions and one broken file cannot abort a run. SPICE requires
Java 8 to 13, so scoring lives in a pinned Linux environment while inference
runs natively; the predictions JSON is the only handoff between the two, and
between laptop and cluster. Cluster compute nodes have no internet access,
which forces fully offline model loads from pre-cached checkpoints and makes
the runs repeatable by construction. Full 1{,}045-clip runs are cheap on one
A100: 8.3 minutes for Qwen2.5-Omni (about 22\,GB VRAM in bf16), 19 minutes
for SALMONN (fp16), and 17.5 minutes for AF3 (about 28\,GB in fp32). Each
LALM receives a fixed one-line instruction asking for one short, factual
sentence; the exact prompt of every row is recorded in its manifest. All
1{,}045 clips completed for every system with zero failures.

\subsection{Systems}
\textbf{AST} (tagging floor): top-5 sigmoid AudioSet labels templated as ``a
sound of \dots''; input window 10.24\,s. \textbf{CNN14+BART}: the official
DCASE 2023 baseline, reproduced at 0.259 SPIDEr-FL against the published
0.261; this reproduction validates the harness. \textbf{EnCLAP-base}
(AudioCaps-pretrained, Clotho-finetuned release): 0.280 SPIDEr-FL in our
harness vs.\ 0.295 SPIDEr published for this configuration~\cite{kim2024enclap}
and 0.291 SPIDEr-FL measured for the released base checkpoint with the same
\texttt{aac-metrics} toolkit~\cite{kim2024enclappp}. The $\approx$1.1\,pp
shortfall (plausibly checkpoint selection or decoding differences) is reported
rather than tuned away; H1 is anchored on CNN14 and unaffected. \textbf{Qwen2.5-Omni-7B}: audio-only,
talker disabled, greedy decoding, bf16. \textbf{SALMONN-13B}: Whisper +
BEATs $\rightarrow$ window-level Q-Former $\rightarrow$ Vicuna-13B, fp16,
beam~4 (its authored default). \textbf{AF3}: Whisper-style encoder +
Qwen2-7B decoder, fp32 (its native precision), greedy. Each LALM uses its
authors' recommended decoding; all decode settings are recorded per row.

\subsection{Polyphony Split (RQ2)}
We derive pseudo-strong labels with PANNs
\texttt{Cnn14\_DecisionLevelMax}~\cite{kong2020panns} (framewise output,
empirically 100\,fps). A clip is \emph{polyphonic} iff two AudioSet classes
are simultaneously active for $\geq$1\,s at confidence $\geq\tau$, evaluated
as the longest co-activation run of the same class pair. The project's public
definition fixed $\tau{=}0.50$; a fallback rule, committed \emph{before} any
subset results were seen, required $\geq$300 clips per bucket and otherwise
selected the largest stored $\tau$ satisfying it. At $\tau{=}0.50$ only 106
clips were polyphonic (609 clips had no activation at all), so the rule
selected $\tau{=}0.25$: \textbf{336 polyphonic / 709 monophonic}. As a
validity check, the five reference captions of SED-polyphonic clips mention
more distinct sound entities than monophonic ones (mean 4.5 vs.\ 3.8;
$\geq$2 entities in 97\% vs.\ 87\% of clips).

\subsection{CHAIR-audio (RQ3)}
Vision CHAIR~\cite{rohrbach2018chair} counts caption objects absent from the
image against a fixed 80-object list. We port this to audio with the 527-label
AudioSet vocabulary (604 surface forms after splitting comma alternatives and
dropping six generic structural forms;
deterministic word-boundary matching with plural/-ing variants; no learned NLP).
An entity in a model caption is \emph{hallucinated} iff it appears neither in
(a) the union of the five reference captions nor (b) the clip's SED tag set at
threshold $\tau$ (dual criterion). We report CHAIR-s (fraction of captions with
$\geq$1 hallucinated entity) at $\tau\in\{0.20,0.25,0.30\}$ per the
preregistered sensitivity bracket, with matcher coverage, and CHAIR-i (fraction
of hallucinated entity mentions) as a descriptive decomposition.

\subsection{Secondary Metric: MACE}
MACE~\cite{dixit2024mace} scores a caption directly against the audio through
CLAP embeddings and applies the FENSE fluency penalty, so it needs no
reference overlap at all. We use the authors' reference implementation with
its MS-CLAP backend. Two implementation properties matter for interpretation:
the backend embeds a randomly cropped 7\,s audio window per call, which makes
repeated runs vary by about 0.002, and its text encoder pads to 77 GPT-2
tokens without truncating, so we clamp candidates to 30 words, which affects
3 of 3{,}135 LALM captions. We treat MACE as a secondary, audio-grounded
check on the reference-based results, not as a headline metric.

\subsection{Preregistered Hypotheses and Statistics}
Hypotheses were preregistered with BCa bootstrap~\cite{efron1987bca}
($n{=}1000$ resamples, seed 42) and Holm--Bonferroni correction:
\textbf{H1}: SPIDEr-FL(AF3) $>$ 0.261 (the published CNN14 baseline;
minimum detectable effect 1.04\,pp);
\textbf{H2}: $\Delta$SPIDEr-FL(poly$-$mono) $>$ 0 per LALM (MDE 1.50\,pp);
\textbf{H3}: AF3 $>$ SALMONN (SPIDEr-FL);
\textbf{H4}: CHAIR(AF3) $<$ CHAIR(SALMONN).
The preregistration was drafted before the LALM runs but never formally
frozen; we therefore treat it as a declared analysis plan and disclose every
deviation (Sec.~\ref{sec:deviations})~\cite{kerr1998harking}. Corpus SPIDEr-FL
equals the mean of per-clip scores exactly, so the bootstrap tests the
reported metric itself.

\subsection{RQ1: Zero-Shot LALMs vs.\ Trained Captioners}
Table~\ref{tab:full} and Fig.~\ref{fig:spider} give the main comparison. The
ordering is AST $\ll$ Qwen2.5-Omni $<$ SALMONN $<$ CNN14 $<$ EnCLAP $<$
\textbf{AF3} (0.068 $\rightarrow$ 0.188 $\rightarrow$ 0.225 $\rightarrow$
0.259 $\rightarrow$ 0.280 $\rightarrow$ 0.297 SPIDEr-FL). Every LALM clears
the tagging floor by a factor of 3--4, so all of them describe scenes rather
than list events. Two of the three nonetheless trail \emph{both} trained
captioners; the blanket claim that zero-shot LALMs beat trained systems is
false. The audio-specialist AF3, however, beats both trained captioners
\emph{on every metric} within the harness, zero-shot; its 0.297 also exceeds
EnCLAP's \emph{published} SPIDEr-FL of 0.291~\cite{kim2024enclappp}, though not
EnCLAP's published CIDEr (0.460 vs.\ 0.461--0.463~\cite{kim2024enclap}).
\textbf{H1 is supported}: mean SPIDEr-FL 0.2968, one-sided
95\% BCa lower bound 0.2828 $>$ 0.261 $+$ MDE (0.2714), $p\approx0.001$,
significant after Holm correction ($\alpha'{=}0.0167$). All LALMs are highly
fluent (FER $\leq$ 0.017; Qwen2.5-Omni's 0.005 is the lowest in the table), so
their differences reflect content, not disfluency. \textbf{H3 is supported}: per-clip paired
AF3$-$SALMONN difference $+$0.072 (CI lower $+$0.058, $p\approx0.001$).

Table~\ref{tab:examples} shows the register differences behind these numbers.
AST emits a tag list. Qwen2.5-Omni wraps its content in report-like framing
(``The audio contains \dots'') and enumerates several events per clip, a style
no Clotho reference uses. SALMONN and the trained captioners produce short
declaratives. AF3 reads closest to the Clotho register, which is precisely
what overlap metrics reward. The second clip also previews the hallucination
analysis: AF3's fluent caption invents a zipper that neither the references
nor the audio tags support.

\subsection{RQ2: Polyphonic vs.\ Monophonic}
Table~\ref{tab:polymono} and Fig.~\ref{fig:polymono}: \emph{all six} systems score higher on the
polyphonic subset. \textbf{H2 is supported for every LALM}: AF3
$\Delta{=}{+}0.088$ (CI lower $+$0.058), Qwen2.5-Omni $+$0.094 ($+$0.071),
SALMONN $+$0.059 ($+$0.032); all $p\approx0.001$ (AF3's $\Delta$, the Holm
family member, passes at $\alpha'{=}0.05$). Because the
trained baselines shift by comparable amounts ($+$0.060 to $+$0.073), this is
a \emph{subset-difficulty} effect rather than an LALM property: event-rich
polyphonic clips give captions more matchable content, while the monophonic
bucket concentrates quiet, ambiguous recordings (219 clips have no SED
activation at $\tau{=}0.25$ at all) whose descriptions legitimately diverge
from references. No LALM shows a polyphony \emph{penalty} relative
to the trained systems at the caption-overlap level. This complements the
event-level degradation with polyphony reported by Harish and
Abe{\ss}er~\cite{harish2025dcase}: counting and naming all events gets harder
with polyphony, but producing a reference-like \emph{description} does not.
The audio-grounded MACE metric independently corroborates the direction on
the LALMs: every poly$-$mono $\Delta$MACE in Table~\ref{tab:mace} is positive,
so the polyphonic advantage is not an artefact of reference overlap.

\subsection{RQ3: Hallucination}
Table~\ref{tab:chair}: at $\tau{=}0.25$, AF3 (0.347), SALMONN (0.332), CNN14
(0.350) and EnCLAP (0.351) cluster tightly, while Qwen2.5-Omni hallucinates
markedly more (0.550) and AST's 0.956 is the expected validity check: an
indiscriminate top-5 tag list almost always contains an unsupported label.
\textbf{H4's null is retained}: AF3 does \emph{not} hallucinate less than
SALMONN (paired difference $-$0.015 at $\tau{=}0.25$, $p{=}0.82$; the sign and
decision are stable across all three thresholds, so the outcome is not
threshold-sensitive). The best captioner by overlap metrics is thus not the
most grounded one; higher SPIDEr-FL is partly earned by richer, more
specific captions that carry more hallucination surface.

Decomposing the rate explains the null. Per caption, AF3 mentions more
vocabulary entities than SALMONN (1.54 vs.\ 1.38 on average; Qwen2.5-Omni
2.08, AST 5.49), and each extra mention is another chance to be unsupported.
Per mention, the picture flips: AF3's CHAIR-i of 0.299 at $\tau{=}0.25$ is
the lowest in the table (SALMONN 0.314, CNN14 0.318, EnCLAP 0.320,
Qwen2.5-Omni 0.437, AST 0.602). AF3 is therefore the most grounded system per
claim it makes; it simply makes more claims per caption, as the zipper example
in Table~\ref{tab:examples} illustrates. We report this as a descriptive
decomposition; H4 was preregistered on the per-caption rate and its verdict
stands. In constrained event
tagging, by contrast, Harish and
Abe{\ss}er~\cite{harish2025dcase} found Qwen2.5-Omni the \emph{more precise}
(less tag-hallucinating) of their two LALMs. Its high
CHAIR here therefore points at unconstrained caption generation
rather than at the model's audio grounding, and its competitive
MACE fits that reading. MACE, which scores
captions against the audio itself, agrees with the ranking split: AF3 leads
(0.593--0.620), but Qwen2.5-Omni ($\approx$0.53--0.56) draws level with
SALMONN despite trailing it by 0.037 SPIDEr-FL. Reference overlap and audio
grounding rank models differently. A concrete failure mode: greedy decoding
drove Qwen2.5-Omni into a 515-word repetition loop (``tapped, tapped,
\dots'') on one clip, caught by the fluency-error metric and visible only
because per-clip outputs are retained.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{What ``zero-shot'' does and does not mean.} Zero-shot here denotes the
evaluation protocol: no task-specific fine-tuning by us and no in-context
examples. It does not mean Clotho-free training: AF3's training corpus lists
the Clotho development pairs (19{,}195 audio--text pairs)~\cite{goel2025af3},
and SALMONN's pre-training and instruction tuning both include
Clotho~\cite{tang2024salmonn}; Qwen2.5-Omni's data mix is
undisclosed~\cite{xu2025qwenomni}. The evaluation split is standard held-out
data for all systems, but prior exposure to the Clotho caption register
plausibly contributes to AF3's overlap-metric advantage (one reason its
captions read Clotho-style). It is also the same development data the trained
baselines were fitted on, so the comparison remains symmetric at the data
level. Harish and Abe{\ss}er~\cite{harish2025dcase} sidestep this by
evaluating on synthetic USM soundscapes; our audio-grounded metrics (CHAIR,
MACE) serve the same role here.
\textbf{Metric scope.} Overlap metrics reward reference-like phrasing; AF3's
Clotho-style captions fit them well. CHAIR-audio and MACE add orthogonal,
audio-grounded axes on which AF3's advantage shrinks or disappears. All three
are needed for a fair picture. (MACE caveat: its MS-CLAP backend randomly
crops a 7\,s window per call, giving run-to-run variation of $\approx$0.002.)
\textbf{Polyphony split.} SED pseudo-labels inherit PANNs' biases; AudioSet
ontology parents (e.g.\ ``Music''/``Musical instrument'') can co-fire and
inflate polyphony. The reference-entity audit supports the split's direction.
The AST cross-check sees only the first 10.24\,s.
\textbf{CHAIR matcher.} The closed vocabulary misses synonyms outside AudioSet
surface forms (coverage $\approx$0.8); this bias applies uniformly to all
models and largely cancels in the paired H4 comparison.
\textbf{Compute.} LALMs ran on an A100; baselines on CPU. Only the compute
location differs; data, scorer, and references are identical.
\textbf{Where this sits.} Harish and Abe{\ss}er's three-level protocol places
captioning at the top of an understanding hierarchy, above tagging and
counting~\cite{harish2025dcase}. Our results fill in the caption level on real
recordings, and the combined picture is consistent: at the event level
polyphony hurts, at the caption level it does not, and hallucination behaves
like a property of free generation rather than of audio grounding. For
evaluation practice this argues for reporting one grounded metric next to
every overlap score, and for stating training-data overlap whenever
``zero-shot'' claims are made on benchmark corpora. As an external
consistency check, AF3's own report lists a Clotho CIDEr of roughly
0.50 under its internal protocol~\cite{goel2025af3}, in the same range as the
0.460 our minimal one-line prompt obtains.

\subsection{Deviations from the Preregistration}\label{sec:deviations}
(1)~The preregistration was drafted but never formally frozen
(\texttt{freeze\_date: null}); we disclose rather than claim confirmatory
status. (2)~RQ2's metric is $\Delta$SPIDEr-FL per the preregistration; a
project-guide reference to ``$\Delta$MACE'' was superseded (and its MACE
description was inaccurate). (3)~H3/H4 were preregistered on an
AudioCaps-single-event set that was never acquired; we substitute paired
per-clip comparisons on Clotho-eval, which are statistically stronger.
(4)~A planned ``CLEAN'' subset was never operationally defined; H1 uses the
full 1{,}045-clip set, the same set anchoring the 0.261 threshold.
(5)~The polyphony detector is PANNs SED rather than the announced PaSST,
because the definition requires frame-level output; the threshold fallback
$\tau{=}0.50\rightarrow0.25$ followed the pre-committed rule. (6)~H2's
``paired'' wording is undefined for disjoint subsets; we use a two-sample
bootstrap. (7)~The preregistration also listed three descriptive-only probes
(temporal ordering, out-of-distribution clips, a silence negative control);
they were cut for scope and never run. No inferential hypothesis depends on
them.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Whether a zero-shot LALM beats trained audio captioners depends on the model:
a current audio-specialist (AF3) does, significantly, on every overlap
metric under our harness and above EnCLAP's published SPIDEr-FL, while a general
omni-model and an older audio LALM do not. The
advantage does not extend to grounding: AF3 hallucinates no less than SALMONN
per caption (although it is the most grounded per mention), and
caption-overlap gains on polyphonic clips are a dataset effect shared by
all systems. Zero-shot captioning quality and audio grounding are separate
axes, and future evaluation should report both. Three follow-ups seem most
useful: temporally strong labels on real recordings, so that event ordering
and counting can be scored next to captions; joint protocols that pair
event-level and caption-level measurements on the same clips, extending the
three-level idea beyond synthetic mixtures; and grounded metrics reported
alongside overlap scores by default.
