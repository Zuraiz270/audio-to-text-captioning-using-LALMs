\title{Zero-Shot Large Audio-Language Models versus Trained Captioners:\\
A Preregistered Evaluation on Clotho with Polyphony and Hallucination Analyses}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Zuraiz}
\IEEEauthorblockA{M.Sc.\ International Software Systems Science, University of Bamberg\\
CH-Proj-M: Master's Project Computational Humanities, SS 2026\\
Supervisor: Prof.\ Dr.-Ing.\ Jakob Abe{\ss}er}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large audio-language models (LALMs) promise open-vocabulary audio understanding
without task-specific training, but whether they match trained audio captioners
remains unclear. We evaluate three zero-shot LALMs (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, SALMONN-13B,
Audio Flamingo 3) against three traditional systems (an AST tagging floor, the
DCASE 2023 CNN14+BART baseline, EnCLAP-base) on the full Clotho~v2.1 evaluation
split under one harness with identical data, decoding provenance, and DCASE
metrics. The trained baselines reproduce their published scores within
0.005 SPIDEr-FL, validating the harness. Zero-shot performance is strongly
model-dependent: two LALMs trail both trained captioners, while Audio Flamingo~3
surpasses them on every metric (SPIDEr-FL 0.297 vs.\ 0.280; bootstrap CI excludes
the preregistered 0.261 threshold). A PANNs-based polyphony split shows
\emph{higher} caption-overlap scores on event-rich polyphonic clips for all six
systems, and a closed-vocabulary hallucination metric (CHAIR-audio) shows the
best captioner does not hallucinate less than its LALM peer. All hypotheses were
preregistered; deviations are disclosed.
Code: \url{https://github.com/Zuraiz270/audio-to-text-captioning-using-LALMs}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Automated audio captioning (AAC) maps an audio recording to a natural-language
description of its sound events. Traditional AAC systems are trained end-to-end
on caption corpora such as Clotho~\cite{drossos2020clotho}; large audio-language
models (LALMs) instead couple a pretrained audio encoder to a large language
model and are used \emph{zero-shot}. Whether zero-shot LALMs already match
purpose-trained captioners --- and where they fail --- is the question this
project answers empirically. We ask:
\textbf{RQ1}: Do zero-shot LALMs match or exceed traditional captioning
baselines on standard metrics (SPIDEr-FL, CIDEr)?
\textbf{RQ2}: How does performance differ between polyphonic (overlapping
events) and monophonic clips?
\textbf{RQ3}: What are the failure modes, in particular caption hallucination?

Our contributions are: (i) a reproducible six-system comparison on the full
Clotho-eval split, anchored by baselines that reproduce their published scores;
(ii) a preregistered, bootstrap-based hypothesis-testing protocol with all
deviations disclosed; (iii) an SED-derived polyphony split and a
closed-vocabulary adaptation of the CHAIR hallucination
metric~\cite{rohrbach2018chair} to audio.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
Clotho~v2.1~\cite{drossos2020clotho} is the standard AAC benchmark: 1{,}045
evaluation clips (15--30\,s, 44.1\,kHz) with five human captions each. The
DCASE challenge series established SPIDEr~\cite{liu2017spider} and its
fluency-penalised variant SPIDEr-FL~\cite{zhou2022fense} as headline metrics;
we compute all metrics with \texttt{aac-metrics}~\cite{labbe_aacmetrics}.
Trained captioners include the DCASE 2023 baseline (CNN14+BART, encoder from
PANNs~\cite{kong2020panns})~\cite{dcase2023task6a} and
EnCLAP~\cite{kim2024enclap}. AST~\cite{gong2021ast} is a 527-class AudioSet
\cite{gemmeke2017audioset} tagger we use as a tagging floor. The LALMs are
Qwen2.5-Omni~\cite{xu2025qwenomni}, SALMONN~\cite{tang2024salmonn}, and Audio
Flamingo~3 (AF3)~\cite{goel2025af3}. For polyphonic scene understanding,
Abe{\ss}er et al.~\cite{abesser2023usm} introduced the USM dataset, and Harish
and Abe{\ss}er~\cite{harish2025dcase} showed LALM performance on
\emph{event-level} tasks degrades with increasing polyphony --- a finding our
\emph{caption-level} RQ2 complements. MACE~\cite{dixit2024mace} scores captions
against the audio itself via CLAP embeddings; we treat it as a secondary metric.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Evaluation Harness}
Every system implements one contract --- \texttt{caption(waveform, sr)
$\rightarrow$ str} --- behind a registry, so a single inference loop and a
single scorer serve all rows. Each run writes a predictions JSON plus a
manifest recording checkpoint SHA-256s, library versions, decode parameters,
and the seed (42). Scoring uses the \texttt{dcase2023} preset of
\texttt{aac-metrics}~0.5.5 (SPIDEr-FL, CIDEr-D, SPICE, METEOR, fluency error)
on identical clip sets; a \texttt{--subset} mechanism restricts scoring to
fixed clip lists for same-set comparisons. Baselines ran on CPU; LALMs ran on
one NVIDIA A100-40GB each (offline, pre-cached checkpoints).

\subsection{Systems}
\textbf{AST} (tagging floor): top-5 sigmoid AudioSet labels templated as ``a
sound of \dots''; input window 10.24\,s. \textbf{CNN14+BART}: the official
DCASE 2023 baseline, reproduced at 0.259 SPIDEr-FL vs.\ the published 0.261.
\textbf{EnCLAP-base}: reproduced at 0.280 vs.\ $\approx$0.283. These two
reproductions validate the harness. \textbf{Qwen2.5-Omni-7B}: audio-only,
talker disabled, greedy decoding, bf16. \textbf{SALMONN-13B}: Whisper +
BEATs $\rightarrow$ window-level Q-Former $\rightarrow$ Vicuna-13B, fp16,
beam~4 (its authored default). \textbf{AF3}: Whisper-style encoder +
Qwen2-7B decoder, fp32 (its native precision), greedy. Each LALM uses its
authors' recommended decoding; all decode settings are recorded per row.

\subsection{Polyphony Split (RQ2)}
We derive pseudo-strong labels with PANNs
\texttt{Cnn14\_DecisionLevelMax}~\cite{kong2020panns} (framewise output,
empirically 100\,fps). A clip is \emph{polyphonic} iff two AudioSet classes
are simultaneously active for $\geq$1\,s at confidence $\geq\tau$, evaluated
as the longest co-activation run of the same class pair. The project's public
definition fixed $\tau{=}0.50$; a fallback rule, committed \emph{before} any
subset results were seen, required $\geq$300 clips per bucket and otherwise
selected the largest stored $\tau$ satisfying it. At $\tau{=}0.50$ only 106
clips were polyphonic (609 clips had no activation at all), so the rule
selected $\tau{=}0.25$: \textbf{336 polyphonic / 709 monophonic}. As a
validity check, the five reference captions of SED-polyphonic clips mention
more distinct sound entities than monophonic ones (mean 4.5 vs.\ 3.8;
$\geq$2 entities in 97\% vs.\ 87\% of clips).

\subsection{CHAIR-audio (RQ3)}
Vision CHAIR~\cite{rohrbach2018chair} counts caption objects absent from the
image against a fixed 80-object list. We port this to audio with the 527-label
AudioSet vocabulary (605 surface forms after splitting comma alternatives;
deterministic word-boundary matching with plural/-ing variants; no learned NLP).
An entity in a model caption is \emph{hallucinated} iff it appears neither in
(a) the union of the five reference captions nor (b) the clip's SED tag set at
threshold $\tau$ (dual criterion). We report CHAIR-s (fraction of captions with
$\geq$1 hallucinated entity) at $\tau\in\{0.20,0.25,0.30\}$ per the
preregistered sensitivity bracket, with matcher coverage.

\subsection{Preregistered Hypotheses and Statistics}
Hypotheses were preregistered with BCa bootstrap~\cite{efron1987bca}
($n{=}1000$ resamples, seed 42) and Holm--Bonferroni correction:
\textbf{H1}: SPIDEr-FL(AF3) $>$ 0.261 (the published CNN14 baseline;
minimum detectable effect 1.04\,pp);
\textbf{H2}: $\Delta$SPIDEr-FL(poly$-$mono) $>$ 0 per LALM (MDE 1.50\,pp);
\textbf{H3}: AF3 $>$ SALMONN (SPIDEr-FL);
\textbf{H4}: CHAIR(AF3) $<$ CHAIR(SALMONN).
The preregistration was drafted before the LALM runs but never formally
frozen; we therefore treat it as a declared analysis plan and disclose every
deviation (Sec.~\ref{sec:deviations})~\cite{kerr1998harking}. Corpus SPIDEr-FL
equals the mean of per-clip scores exactly, so the bootstrap tests the
reported metric itself.

\subsection{RQ1: Zero-Shot LALMs vs.\ Trained Captioners}
Table~\ref{tab:full} and Fig.~\ref{fig:spider} give the main comparison. The
ordering is AST $\ll$ Qwen2.5-Omni $<$ SALMONN $<$ CNN14 $<$ EnCLAP $<$
\textbf{AF3} (0.068 $\rightarrow$ 0.188 $\rightarrow$ 0.225 $\rightarrow$
0.259 $\rightarrow$ 0.280 $\rightarrow$ 0.297 SPIDEr-FL). Three observations.
(1)~Every LALM clears the tagging floor by 3--4$\times$: LALMs describe scenes
rather than list events. (2)~Two of three LALMs trail \emph{both} trained
captioners, so ``zero-shot LALMs beat trained systems'' is false in general.
(3)~The audio-specialist AF3 beats both trained captioners \emph{on every
metric}, zero-shot. \textbf{H1 is supported}: mean SPIDEr-FL 0.2968, one-sided
95\% BCa lower bound 0.2828 $>$ 0.261 $+$ MDE (0.2714), $p\approx0.001$,
significant after Holm correction ($\alpha'{=}0.0167$). All LALMs are highly
fluent (FER $\leq$ 0.017, lowest in the table), so their differences reflect
content, not disfluency. \textbf{H3 is supported}: per-clip paired
AF3$-$SALMONN difference $+$0.072 (CI lower $+$0.058, $p\approx0.001$).

\subsection{RQ2: Polyphonic vs.\ Monophonic}
Table~\ref{tab:polymono}: \emph{all six} systems score higher on the
polyphonic subset. \textbf{H2 is supported for every LALM} --- AF3
$\Delta{=}{+}0.088$ (CI lower $+$0.058), Qwen2.5-Omni $+$0.094 ($+$0.071),
SALMONN $+$0.059 ($+$0.032); all $p\approx0.001$, Holm-corrected. Because the
trained baselines shift by comparable amounts ($+$0.060 to $+$0.073), this is
a \emph{subset-difficulty} effect rather than an LALM property: event-rich
polyphonic clips give captions more matchable content, while the monophonic
bucket concentrates quiet, ambiguous recordings (219 clips have no SED
activation at $\tau{=}0.25$ at all) whose descriptions legitimately diverge
from references. Notably, no LALM shows a polyphony \emph{penalty} relative
to the trained systems at the caption-overlap level --- complementing the
event-level degradation with polyphony reported by Harish and
Abe{\ss}er~\cite{harish2025dcase}: counting and naming all events gets harder
with polyphony, but producing a reference-like \emph{description} does not.
The audio-grounded MACE metric~\cite{dixit2024mace} (reference implementation,
MS-CLAP backend) independently corroborates the direction on the LALMs:
poly$-$mono $\Delta$MACE is $+$0.028 (AF3), $+$0.021 (SALMONN), and $+$0.032
(Qwen2.5-Omni).

\subsection{RQ3: Hallucination}
Table~\ref{tab:chair}: at $\tau{=}0.25$, AF3 (0.347), SALMONN (0.332), CNN14
(0.350) and EnCLAP (0.351) cluster tightly, while Qwen2.5-Omni hallucinates
markedly more (0.550) and AST's 0.956 is the expected validity check --- an
indiscriminate top-5 tag list almost always contains an unsupported label.
\textbf{H4's null is retained}: AF3 does \emph{not} hallucinate less than
SALMONN (paired difference $-$0.015 at $\tau{=}0.25$, $p{=}0.82$; the sign and
decision are stable across all three thresholds, so the outcome is not
threshold-sensitive). The best captioner by overlap metrics is thus not the
most grounded one --- higher SPIDEr-FL is partly earned by richer, more
specific captions that carry more hallucination surface. MACE, which scores
captions against the audio itself, agrees with the ranking split: AF3 leads
(0.593--0.620), but Qwen2.5-Omni ($\approx$0.53--0.56) draws level with
SALMONN despite trailing it by 0.037 SPIDEr-FL --- reference overlap and audio
grounding rank models differently. A concrete failure mode: greedy decoding
drove Qwen2.5-Omni into a 515-word repetition loop (``tapped, tapped,
\dots'') on one clip --- caught by the fluency-error metric and visible only
because per-clip outputs are retained.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Metric scope.} Overlap metrics reward reference-like phrasing; AF3's
Clotho-style captions fit them well. CHAIR-audio and MACE add orthogonal,
audio-grounded axes on which AF3's advantage shrinks or disappears. All three
are needed for a fair picture. (MACE caveat: its MS-CLAP backend randomly
crops a 7\,s window per call, giving run-to-run variation of $\approx$0.002.)
\textbf{Polyphony split.} SED pseudo-labels inherit PANNs' biases; AudioSet
ontology parents (e.g.\ ``Music''/``Musical instrument'') can co-fire and
inflate polyphony. The reference-entity audit supports the split's direction.
The AST cross-check sees only the first 10.24\,s.
\textbf{CHAIR matcher.} The closed vocabulary misses synonyms outside AudioSet
surface forms (coverage $\approx$0.8); this bias applies uniformly to all
models and largely cancels in the paired H4 comparison.
\textbf{Compute.} LALMs ran on an A100; baselines on CPU. Only the compute
location differs --- data, scorer, and references are identical.

\subsection{Deviations from the Preregistration}\label{sec:deviations}
(1)~The preregistration was drafted but never formally frozen
(\texttt{freeze\_date: null}); we disclose rather than claim confirmatory
status. (2)~RQ2's metric is $\Delta$SPIDEr-FL per the preregistration; a
project-guide reference to ``$\Delta$MACE'' was superseded (and its MACE
description was inaccurate). (3)~H3/H4 were preregistered on an
AudioCaps-single-event set that was never acquired; we substitute paired
per-clip comparisons on Clotho-eval, which are statistically stronger.
(4)~A planned ``CLEAN'' subset was never operationally defined; H1 uses the
full 1{,}045-clip set --- the same set anchoring the 0.261 threshold.
(5)~The polyphony detector is PANNs SED rather than the announced PaSST,
because the definition requires frame-level output; the threshold fallback
$\tau{=}0.50\rightarrow0.25$ followed the pre-committed rule. (6)~H2's
``paired'' wording is undefined for disjoint subsets; we use a two-sample
bootstrap.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Whether a zero-shot LALM beats trained audio captioners depends on the model:
a current audio-specialist (AF3) does, significantly and on every overlap
metric, while a general omni-model and an older audio LALM do not. The
advantage does not extend to grounding: AF3 hallucinates no less than SALMONN,
and caption-overlap gains on polyphonic clips are a dataset effect shared by
all systems. Zero-shot captioning quality and audio grounding are separate
axes --- future evaluation should report both.
