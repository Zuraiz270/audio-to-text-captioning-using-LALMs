## Ekphrasis

**Tradition.** Classical rhetoric → 20th-century literary theory.
**Originating thinker(s).** Homer (*Shield of Achilles*, Iliad XVIII); Philostratus the Elder; W. J. T. Mitchell 1986; James A. W. Heffernan 1993.

**Definition.** Ekphrasis is the rhetorical genre of **verbal description of non-verbal aesthetic experience**. Originally the description of visual art (the carved shield, the painted urn) within a literary text, it has been extended in modern theory to any inter-modal translation: word about image, word about sound, word about embodied experience.

**Why it matters here.** Automated Audio Captioning is the **computational instantiation of ekphrasis for acoustic objects**: the input is non-verbal aesthetic experience (a soundscape, a recorded scene), the output is grammatical text describing it. Framing AAC as ekphrasis does three things:

1. It supplies a 2,500-year humanities lineage to a problem that engineering literature treats as merely a benchmark task — this is the project's claim to belong in Computational Humanities, not engineering.
2. It surfaces the **representational problem** behind the failure modes ([polyphony-under-description](../06_failure_modes/polyphony-under-description.md), [entity-hallucination](../06_failure_modes/entity-hallucination.md), [temporal-grounding-loss](../06_failure_modes/temporal-grounding-loss.md)) — these are not engineering bugs but failures of inter-modal translation that ekphrastic theory has long catalogued (the "ekphrastic fear" of inadequacy, the substitution of convention for description).
3. It gives the project a critical apparatus — Mitchell, Heffernan, Sterne — that can pull humanistic interpretation out of quantitative results.
